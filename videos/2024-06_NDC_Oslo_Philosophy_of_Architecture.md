# NDC Oslo 2024 - The Philosophy of Architecture

## The Philosophy of Architecture

*Barry O'Reilly (former Chief Architect at Microsoft, author of Residuality Theory)*

[YouTube][video] · [NDC Oslo agenda][ndc]

> This session looks at the underlying philosophical beliefs that inform the everyday practice of software architecture. Instead of endlessly arguing in terms of frameworks and methods, why not understand why we think the way we do, why some people disagree, and why we can't trust our Computer Science educations? Who are we, why are we like this, and can we ever change?

The talk is a step back from frameworks and methods: it looks at the beliefs about the world that architects bring to their work, often without noticing them.

### Kuhn — The Structure of Scientific Revolutions

- A **paradigm** is the set of assumptions, methods and exemplary problems a community shares. It defines what counts as a valid question and a valid answer.
- **Normal science** happens *inside* a paradigm: solving puzzles without questioning the frame.
- **Anomalies** pile up until the paradigm can no longer absorb them. This leads to a crisis, then a **revolution** (a *paradigm shift*).
- Paradigms are **incommensurable**: people in different paradigms argue past each other because they don't share the same criteria.

💡 This helps explain why architects disagree endlessly about methods: they often don't share the same underlying paradigm. The debate is really about worldviews.

### Baudrillard — Simulacra and Simulation

- **Simulacra** are copies with no original. Representations end up replacing the reality they were supposed to describe.
- There are successive stages of the image: it reflects reality, then distorts it, then hides the absence of reality, then has no relation to reality at all (pure simulacrum).
- **Hyperreality**: the model becomes more "real" than the territory.
- Borges' map, which ends up covering the whole empire, is the opening image of the book.

💡 For architecture: our diagrams, models and frameworks can turn into simulacra. We end up working on the model rather than on the real (complex, unpredictable) system and its environment.

### Linear and Lateral Thinking

| Linear thinking | Lateral thinking |
|---|---|
| George Polya, *How to Solve It*: "a system of thinking to help you solve any problem" | Edward de Bono, *Lateral Thinking: An Introduction* |
| Step-by-step method: understand the problem, make a plan, carry out the plan, look back | Deliberately leave the obvious path to look at the problem from unexpected angles (provocation, random entry points) |
| Works well for well-defined problems | Useful when the problem itself is poorly understood |

### Deleuze — Difference and Repetition: the architectural walk

- Reference: Gilles Deleuze, *Différence et Répétition* (1968).
- Deleuze puts **difference** first, rather than identity: things aren't copies of a stable model. Each repetition brings something new.
- **Architectural walk**: going over the architecture again and again. Each pass isn't an identical repeat: it shows differences and new aspects of the system.

### Why we can't trust our CS education

- Computer Science education tends to pass on a mechanistic, reductionist worldview implicitly: the world can be decomposed, modelled and predicted.
- This works for the software itself, but not for the business and social environment the software lives in. That environment is complex and uncertain.
- ⚠️ Since this worldview is never made explicit, it is hard to question. You first have to *see* the paradigm before you can change it, which is the point of the talk.

### Concepts
- **Paradigm / paradigm shift** (Kuhn): the shared frame of a scientific community, and how it gets replaced through a revolution
- **Incommensurability** (Kuhn): competing paradigms cannot be fully compared using shared criteria
- **Simulacrum / hyperreality** (Baudrillard): a representation that has replaced the reality it refers to
- **Linear vs lateral thinking** (Polya / de Bono): step-by-step problem solving vs indirect, creative reframing
- **Difference and repetition** (Deleuze): repetition always produces difference. This is the basis of the "architectural walk"
- **Positivism / reductionism**: the belief that the world can be known through decomposition, measurement and prediction
- **Residuality Theory** (O'Reilly): designing architectures by stressing them against random stressors rather than trying to predict the future
